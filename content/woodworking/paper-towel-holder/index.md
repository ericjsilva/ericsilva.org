## Project Overview

A countertop paper towel holder with a New Mexico Zia symbol carved into the base. The base is built from alternating strips of two contrasting red oak tones, giving the round top a striped pattern that radiates out from the CNC-carved Zia in the center.

## Inspiration

I wanted a paper towel holder with some personality instead of a plain base, and since I live in New Mexico, the Zia symbol felt like an appropriate design to enhance the project.

## Materials

- 3/4" x 1.5" red oak (two contrasting color tones)
- 1" red oak dowel
- Wood glue

## Tools Used

- Table saw with miter gauge for cutting the strips to length
- Clamps for the glue-up
- Planer
- Circle cutting jig and table saw
- Router table with a 45 degree chamfer bit and a roundover bit
- CNC with a 60 degree V-bit
- 1" Forstner bit and drill press
- 120 and 220 sandpaper

## Build Notes

1. Crosscut the two 4' red oak boards into 7.5" lengths.
2. Alternate the two colors and glue the pieces together edge to edge to form a rough 7.5" square panel.
3. Run the glued-up panel through the planer to bring it down to a final thickness of 1.25". The panel is only 7.5" long, shorter than my planer's 12" minimum feed length, so run it on a sled instead of feeding it bare. I built mine from a piece of MDF with a strip of scrap 2x4 ripped down and glued to the back edge as a stop for the panel. The sled itself is longer than 12", so the rollers stay engaged the whole time and the panel can't drop into the cutterhead gap.
4. Use a circle cutting jig on the table saw to cut the panel into a circle.
5. Rout a 45 degree chamfer around the top edge on the router table to profile the rim.
6. Carve the New Mexico Zia symbol into the top face on the CNC using a 60 degree V-bit.
7. Bore the dowel recess in the center with a 1" Forstner bit.
8. Cut the red oak dowel to 14" and round over one end on the router table.
9. Sand the base and dowel with 220 grit.

## Finishing

- Two coats of tung oil

## Lessons Learned

- Alternating the two oak tones before the glue-up is what makes the Zia carving pop once the circle is cut, since the rings line up with the segment lines.
- Cutting the dowel recess with a Forstner bit after the top is chamfered made it easier to keep the bit centered.
